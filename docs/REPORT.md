# Freshness Lab — Experiment report (HOST SIMULATION, exploratory/pilot)

**Everything in this report is a host simulation** (`build/flsim`, x86-64,
gcc 13.3.0). No board, no radio, no real clock. Host runtime is reported only
as host runtime. Bytes are bytes, not energy. This is the **first, exploratory
/ pilot matrix**: it was run on development seeds and is not a held-out
evaluation (docs/DESIGN.md §10.1). No superiority claim is made anywhere in
this document; negative results are reported as measured.

All tables and headline numbers below were generated by a script from the
files under `results/` (they are not transcribed by hand); the full per-cell
tables are in `results/matrix/summary.md`.

## 2. Definitions used below (docs/DESIGN.md §7.3, §8.5, §8.6)

* `recall` = receiver-delivered unique events / **all generated** events
  (rejected and undelivered ones count against it). `on_time_rate` =
  deliveries with `rx_time <= deadline_abs` / all generated. Late deliveries
  are never counted as on time.
* Sender terminal outcomes (`REJECTED_FULL`, `ACKED`, `RETRY_EXHAUSTED`,
  `RETENTION_EXPIRED`, `PENDING`) are exclusive and sum to `ev_generated` on
  every run (checked). `delivered_but_unacked` = receiver delivered it, sender
  never got the ACK.
* **AoI is conditional.** `aoi_mean_ms` is the continuous-time mean age of the
  receiver's snapshot, per stream from that stream's *first reception* to the
  end of the run, averaged over streams. It is `NA` if any configured stream
  never received a snapshot, and must always be read together with
  `unknown_ms_sum` (time before first reception, summed over streams) and
  `aoi_defined_streams`: a late first reception shortens the averaging
  interval and can lower the conditional mean while the receiver actually knew
  less. In this pilot every stream in every run received at least one snapshot
  (`aoi_defined_streams` equals the configured stream count everywhere), so no
  `NA` cells occur.
* `bytes_total_tx` charges every transmitted frame, lost or not, both
  directions.

## 3. The demonstration: alarm raised and cleared inside an outage

Scenario `alarm_outage`, seed 101, policy `edf_rr` (the state-side story is
identical for every policy; the event side differs by at most one 10 ms slot).
Stream 0 (`alarm_active`) publishes 0 every second, 1 at 10000 and 11000, and
0 again from 12000. Event #1 RAISE is generated at 10000 (deadline 11000,
retention 27000), event #2 CLEAR at 12000 (deadline 13000, retention 29000).
Both link directions lose everything for 9000 ≤ t < 15000.

**Latest-state side.** The receiver's applied snapshots of stream 0 around the
outage (from `rx.csv`):

| t (ms) | applied seq | generated at | value |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 7020 | 8 | 7000 | 0 |
| 8020 | 9 | 8000 | 0 |
| 15030 | 16 | 15000 | 0 |

Seq 9 (value 0, generated 8000) is the last snapshot applied before the
outage; the next applied snapshot is seq 16 (value 0, generated 15000). The
two snapshots carrying value 1 (seq 11 generated at 10000 and seq 12 at
11000) were each transmitted three times into the outage (10200–10800 and
11100–11700 in `decisions.csv`), lost every time, and then replaced as the
stream's latest snapshot by the value-0 snapshots generated from 12000 on;
nothing carrying value 1 ever reached the receiver.
Stream 0 published 30 snapshots, transmitted 44 frames (retries during the
outage) and had 24 applied; its AoI peaked at 7030 ms, spent
5030 ms above the 2000 ms threshold, was unknown for the first 20 ms,
and its conditional mean AoI is 1222.5 ms. **The receiver never sees
`alarm_active = 1`.** A latest-state-only system has no record that the alarm
fired.

**Event-ledger side** (`events.csv`, both ends merged by the host):

| id | kind | gen | deadline | retention | outcome | terminal t | attempts | first tx | rx first | on time | latency |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | RAISE | 10000 | 11000 | 27000 | ACKED | 15140 | 18 | 10000 | 15120 | 0 | 5120 |
| 2 | CLEAR | 12000 | 13000 | 29000 | ACKED | 15040 | 11 | 12000 | 15020 | 0 | 3020 |

Both occurrences are delivered, **0 of 2 on time** (deadline misses of
4020 ms and 2020 ms), with their immutable IDs and generation times intact:
recall 2/2, on-time 0/2. Their arrival order is **reversed**: CLEAR #2
(generated 12000) reaches the receiver at 15020, RAISE #1 (generated 10000) at
15120. The reason is visible in the decision log: at 15000 the RAISE was still
in flight (attempt 17, sent at 14800 into the outage, ACK timeout 300 ms), so
only the CLEAR was eligible; the RAISE became eligible again at 15100. EDF
orders eligible records, not in-flight ones.

**What this does and does not show.** The event log preserves the history of
both occurrences with correct generation times. It does **not** establish that
a receiver would reconstruct the correct alarm state from reordered
deliveries: no such application logic exists in v0.1, and an application that
applied the two occurrences in arrival order would end in the wrong state
(RAISE last). Ordering by generation time is the application's job and is
future work.

**Retry-budget negative finding (recorded, docs/DESIGN.md §10.2).** The first
pilot of this scenario used the common `max_attempts=8`. With a 300 ms ACK
timeout that is 2.1 s of retries, so both events ended `RETRY_EXHAUSTED`
inside the outage (RAISE at 12400 after 8 attempts, CLEAR at 14400) and
`rx_ev_delivered` was 0: the declared 17 s retention was unreachable. The
deterministic re-run of that pilot is retained under
`results/pilot/alarm_outage_seed101_max_attempts8/`. The two outage scenarios
now use `max_attempts=40` for every policy.

## 4. Explicit rejection: `overflow` (seed 101, `edf_rr`)

Twenty RAISEs arrive at 5100..7000 while both directions are down
(5000 ≤ t < 12000); `FL_EVENT_CAPACITY` is 8. From `events.csv`: generated
40, **rejected at admission 12** (IDs 9..20, each burning its ID), delivered
eventually **28/40**, on time **20/40**, late 8 (the eight admitted
RAISEs, delivered after the outage with 22–25 attempts each). The 20
CLEARs generated after the outage are all on time. Every policy produces the
same recall 0.7 and on-time rate 0.5 on every seed (verified from
`summary_by_scenario.csv`): the loss here is a capacity decision made at
admission, not a scheduling one.

## 7. Findings (measured; with the regressions that pin them)

1. **Near parity where the link is healthy.** In `healthy_light`,
   `burst_loss`, `ack_loss` and `reorder` every policy delivers 100 % of
   events on time; mean AoI differs by under 1 % between policies (e.g.
   `healthy_light` 279.7 vs 279.7 ms). The candidate offers no
   benefit beyond that near parity in these pilot runs, and its measured link traffic in those scenarios
   is comparable to the baselines' (`bytes_total_tx` within about 1 %); no
   CPU or energy cost was measured and none is claimed. `ack_loss` (40 % reverse loss)
   costs about 1.40× the bytes of `healthy_light` for the same outcome, and
   `reorder` shows the receiver dropping stale STATE frames without regressing
   (`rx_state_stale_dropped` in `summary_all.csv`).
2. **Overload is the trade-off, and the candidate loses on events.** In
   `overload` (a RAISE every 8 ms for 20 s, capacity 8) `fresh` has lower recall
   (0.770 vs 0.782) and on-time rate (0.762 vs 0.774) than
   `edf_rr`, and rejects more events at admission (575 vs 544), while its
   mean AoI drops from 6232 to 1210 ms and the time above the 2 s threshold from
   70.6 s to 26.9 s (summed over 4 streams). `fresh_so` (starvation
   override) pays more events (0.764) for 716 ms; `fifo` has the best AoI
   (182 ms) and the worst recall (0.658). This contradicts the design note that
   the candidate would degenerate to `fresh_nodefer` under overload (corrected
   in docs/DESIGN.md §9.3): in-flight events are not eligible, so the
   "three eligible events" urgency condition is often false. The recall gap
   is about 1.2 percentage points. The reduced
   counterexample (§8) shows the mechanism of the event loss.
3. **Late demotion moves failures, it does not remove them.** `edf_rr_ld` in
   `overload` cuts late deliveries from 20.4 to 2.4 per run but 40
   events expire at retention and 22 are **delivered but never
   acknowledged**: demoted late events go out in idle slots so close to their
   retention that the ACK returns after the sender expired them. The two
   ledgers legitimately disagree, and the host reports both.
4. **The scenario built to make the candidate lose did not.** In
   `tight_deadline` (8 streams, 1.2 s outages every 3 s, events right after
   each outage, 20 % loss) the on-time rate is 0.947 for `fresh` vs 0.937 for
   `edf_rr` (seed ranges 0.894737–0.973684 vs 0.921053–0.973684), and
   `fifo` is highest at 0.963. The ranges overlap; with five pilot seeds no
   ordering is claimed. The deferral mechanism the scenario targets does exist:
   the reducer's author hand-built a smaller variant (8 stale streams, one
   event after the outage, loss on the deferred slot) on which `fresh` misses
   and `edf_rr` succeeds, but that case is a construction, not a matrix
   result, and is not retained as evidence.
5. **`alarm_outage` and `overflow`** behave as described in §3–§4 for every
   policy: on-time 0/2 and 20/40, recall 2/2 and 28/40.
6. **Retry budget × ACK timeout bounds an event's life** regardless of
   retention (§3, docs/DESIGN.md §10.2).
7. **Bounded memory:** `sizeof(fl_sender_t)` = 888 B and `sizeof(fl_receiver_t)`
   = 296 B for the default capacities on x86-64 (`docs/MEMORY.md`); no heap
   in `core/`.

## 8. Counterexample reduction (`results/reduce/overload_seed101_recall/`)

`tools/reduce_counterexample.py` was run on `overload`, seed 101, predicate
`fresh.recall < edf_rr.recall`, with the trace, config and time axis fixed:

* full workload 2980 rows: baseline recall 0.7808, candidate 0.7668;
* reduced to **13 rows** in 148 flsim runs, 1-minimal: yes;

Mechanism (from `walkthrough.md`): twelve RAISEs arrive every 8 ms; at slot
619 (t = 6190) `fresh` serves `STATE s3 STATE_STARVATION` because stream 3 has
just published for the first time and a never-transmitted stream counts as
starved (docs/DESIGN.md §9.0: `last_tx_time == NONE`). That single deferred
slot shifts every later event by one slot, so the twelfth RAISE arrives while
all eight slots are still occupied and is `REJECTED_FULL`. `edf_rr` never
defers and admits all twelve. The trade-off is real and the rule that
triggers it is a design choice, now documented.

## 9. Review reconciliation

Three findings came out of the adversarial code review (three lenses, each
finding challenged by two independent skeptics):

* *Receiver/reverse-service tests missing* — refuted (superseded: the suites
  `tests/test_state_rx.c`, `test_events.c`, `test_frame_time.c`,
  `test_policy.c` exist and pass).
* *Programmatic trace delay could wrap `t + delay` into a past bucket so a
  transmitted frame vanished uncounted* — confirmed, fixed: `sim_run` now
  rejects DATA and ACK delays above `FL_MAX_REL_MS`, and fails the run unless
  every transmitted frame is delivered, lost or in transit (regression in
  `tests/test_sim.c`, including the wrap inputs).
* *A never-received stream contributed a numeric 0 to the cross-stream AoI
  mean* — confirmed, fixed: `aoi_mean_ms` is `NA` unless every stream was
  observed; the partial aggregate is labelled `aoi_mean_defined_ms` with
  `aoi_defined_streams`; the summarizer marks a seed group with any `NA` as
  unavailable rather than averaging the rest, the plot draws an explicit `NA`
  marker, the reducer never compares an undefined metric, and a numeric 0 is
  never treated as missing (regressions: two-stream, all-unknown,
  `[50, NA]` vs `[60, 60]`, numeric zero).

Independent verifiers of the tool builders (generators, matrix runner,
reducer) accepted all three; they also found that the Python scenario table
disagreed with the committed config files on `max_attempts` (fixed, now
tested), and a stale-object build hazard surfaced during integration (fixed:
header dependencies are generated). Two reducer notes are recorded rather
than fixed in this milestone: argparse usage errors and the "no
counterexample" outcome both exit with code 2, and the final
baseline/candidate pair runs outside `--max-runs` (documented in
`reduction.json` as `final_runs_not_counted`).

## 11. Limitations

* Host simulation only; MCU timing, power and flash have not been measured
  (hardware is unavailable and outside the project's scope).
* Exploratory/pilot seeds; no held-out evaluation; five seeds per cell.
* Channel model: per-slot independent or two-state loss and fixed per-slot
  delay; no corruption, partial frames or clock drift.
* Reverse ACK service is one frame per slot with a 4-entry event ring.
* Sender knowledge is last-ACKed metadata only; no estimator.
* Dedup window of 64 IDs; out-of-window IDs are refused (never exercised in
  this matrix: `rx_ev_out_of_window` is 0 everywhere).
* No receiver-visible loss notification; accounting is local to each end.
* The alarm demo shows history preservation, not correct reordered
  alarm-state application.

## 12. Scope and future work (software-only)

This project is software-only: hardware is unavailable and outside its
scope, so MCU timing, power and flash have not been measured and no board
has run this code. That is a factual limitation of the evidence, not a
pending task. Future work, none of it implemented, would stay in software:
an unseen-seed evaluation after freezing the policies and configurations;
workload and budget sensitivity (event rate, deadline, retry budget, buffer
capacity); stronger adversarial fault combinations (overlapping outages,
ACK-only loss during overload, reordering under overload); and CRC plus
corruption in the channel model. A short summary for new readers is in
`docs/RESULTS_SUMMARY.md`.
